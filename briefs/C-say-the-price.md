# Creator Script Ad — Concept C: "Say the price"

**Product:** Rollr refillable roll-on deodorant · **Format:** 9:16, **31–45s by variation**, single creator, phone-shot
**Batch:** "Buy well, buy once" (3 of 3) · **Borrowed vehicle:** Finisterre "Barents jumper" (IG `Dc_Gjy9McN5`, 2026-09-07)

---

### Strategy

Rollr's price-defence work is already seventeen hooks deep and all of it argues that £45 is *worth it*; none of it changes what the £45 is being compared against. This ad says the price in the first two words, concedes the objection out loud ("my old three-quid one worked fine"), and then does the one thing the reference ad failed to do — puts the arithmetic on screen, with units, so the viewer sees a one-off sitting next to a repeat purchase. The belief-reframe: **you think you're comparing £45 to £3; you're actually comparing £45 once to £3 forever.**

---

### Visual Reference

**The borrow, named:** this is a **vehicle / format borrow** from Finisterre's Barents jumper ad — an **indirect, non-competing** brand (Cornish knitwear, no category, intent or auction overlap), which is the permitted side of the panel's §4 line. What we take: cold-open mid-action, received wisdom stated flat then converted to first-person proof, a macro evidence passage in the middle third, resolve on habit rather than a hard sell, phrase-chunked karaoke captions running independently of the cuts, handheld single-talent natural light. What we do **not** take: a single word of their copy, their proverb, or their open-the-frame-and-leave-it-empty ending. Finisterre says "built to last for years" and shows a visibly new jumper. We show the maths.

**Two deliberate departures from the reference:**
1. **Frame 1 is eyes to lens.** The other two ads in this batch hide the face (A opens on a refill-click macro, B on a cupboard). This one is the pattern interrupt of *candour* — a person looking straight at you and naming the price before you can. The reference's face-obscured open is the thing we trade away on purpose, and it is the main reason this ad survives the mute test against its siblings.
2. **Captions sit in the lower third, not mid-frame.** Both independent reads of the reference flagged its mid-frame cards sitting over the product as a weakness.

**Look:** urban-neutral, not domestic. Concrete, steel, poured-resin or dark stone worktop; grey north-facing daylight; no bathroom tiles, no cupboard, no plants, no candles. Fast cutting — roughly 7 picture segments in 20s against the reference's 6 in 17.5s, and the maths section cuts faster still.

### Creator Direction

**Tone.** Flat, dry, slightly amused at themselves. The register is "mate explaining a purchase they've already made peace with", not "creator doing an ad read". No rising intonation at the end of sentences. No "you guys". No smiling into the first line — the smile, if it comes, comes at the end. Rollr's house voice self-deprecates before the customer can; do that, then stop and give the number.

**Setting.** One location, one session. A kitchen or studio worktop in hard grey daylight, plus one standing position against a plain wall. Nothing styled. The worktop should look like a surface someone works on, not a flat-lay.

**Wardrobe.** Plain dark crew-neck or shirt, no logos, no pattern, no jewellery that rattles on the mic. The product is the only object with colour in frame.

**Casting.** Any gender, 28–45, UK accent, someone who reads as having opinions about objects. Not glossy. The reference ad's casting did its aspirational work for it; ours has to do the opposite — this person must look like they'd query a bill.

**Dos**
- Say the price as the first thing out of your mouth, before any greeting, context or brand name.
- Put the bottle down hard enough to hear the glass on the worktop. That sound is the hook's second half.
- Hold the generic stick and the bottle in the same hand at least once, so the comparison is physical.
- Let the maths cards do the talking — don't read every number aloud. Say the argument, show the figures.
- Concede. "My old one worked fine" must sound like you mean it, because you do.

**Don'ts**
- Don't say "obsessed", "game changer", "I'm not gonna lie", or "hear me out".
- Don't name or show a competitor brand. The second stick is an **unbranded generic plain white/grey stick**, label removed or turned away — same construction the existing S02 comparison assets use, and the defensible one.
- Don't mention sweat, dryness, staying dry, or anything that sounds like an antiperspirant claim.
- Don't talk about the gemstone, the scent, or how beautiful it is. That ground is spent — 38 of 187 shipped hooks already live there.
- Don't quote a review, a rating, an award or a patent. None of those are confirmed for this batch.
- Don't improvise a number. Every figure in this script is either locked or bracketed; if a bracket is still empty on shoot day, shoot the line both ways (see **Numbers used**).

### UGC Script

> **Runtime and pace — read this before changing a line.** Target **31–45s by variation**, 9:16. The measured natural delivery rate for this material is **~160 wpm** — relaxed, conversational, with air around the lines, which is what a candour concept needs: *"I'm not going to pretend that's cheap"* wants a beat after it, and at a brisk 230 wpm it does not get one. (The reference ad runs 230 wpm; that is a ceiling, and it is not what this concept should sound like.) Every frame timestamp below is derived from word count at 160 wpm plus breath, not authored by feel. **No single shot runs longer than ~3.5s** — see the Shot list, where each Frame is broken into beats. **The words are the fixed thing here. If this needs to be longer, add shots; do not cut lines.**

Three variations. **Two builds, stated explicitly, not implied:**

- **C1 and C3 are a re-hook pair.** They share Frames 3–7 — same setups, same spine, same edit — and differ only in the hook line and the hook visual (bottle set down hard vs. generic stick held up). Shoot them back to back in the same session off one spine. This is deliberate, cheap, and legitimate re-hooking.
- **The "small British brand" line sits in the C1/C3 spine, which means it lands in both — deliberately.** It opens Frame 7, between the maths resolving and the last line, where it reframes £45 as a decision somebody made rather than a number somebody set. At the longer runtime it now gets its own two shots and a beat of silence in front of it, which is what the line was always worth.
- **C2 is a separate build, and the longer runtime makes that call easier, not harder.** Its written-card device runs the full length: at ~34s the maths accumulates properly — each number landing on its own insert with its own beat — instead of three figures being rushed past in nine seconds. The card is also the single richest coverage source in the batch (overhead, profile, held to camera, and an insert per number as it is written), which is exactly what the expanded shot list needs. **The round-3 call stands and is strengthened:** C2 does not share the spine.

### Shot list

One creator, one session, now roughly **three hours** — the longer runtime is paid for in coverage, not in performance. Phone on a tripod or propped; a second pair of hands is now genuinely useful. Natural light only. Record a scratch audio pass for sync and a clean VO pass afterwards.

**The governing rule: no shot holds longer than ~3.5 seconds.** A six-second hold on a talking head is where attention dies, and both reads of the reference put its likely drop points at its slowest stretches — which were only 2.5s. ### Ad caption

**Primary**
> We've never once pretended this is cheap. It's £45, it's a deodorant, and we know exactly how that sounds.
>
> What we'd rather you compared is the shape of the spend. The glass bottle is a one-off. Everything after it is powder in a paper sachet. The other one is a thing you buy, and buy, and buy.
>
> Three scents. Mandarin & Cedarwood, Vetiver & Lemon, Clary Sage & Rosemary.

**Alternate A — concession-first**
> If your current deodorant works fine, it works fine. We're not here to tell you otherwise.
>
> We're here about the other half of the sum: you'll put deodorant on 22,000 times in your life, and nobody prices it like that. £45, once, then refills. Have a look and decide for yourself.

**Alternate B — short**
> £45. Once. Then powder.
>
> You'll put deodorant on 22,000 times in your life — we thought the bottle may as well be the same one each time.

### Claims check

Walked line by line against `constraints.md` §4 and the `claims.watch` families in `copy.md` §4b.

**Banned outright — all clear.**
- "Medical grade" / "clinical grade": **absent.**
- Hormone / endocrine / oestrogen claims: **absent.**
- "Lymphatic drainage": **absent.**
- Absolute "stops odour": **absent.** This script makes **no efficacy claim of any kind** — it does not say the product works. That is deliberate: the ad's whole argument is about the shape of the spend, and C3 explicitly concedes that the viewer's current deodorant works. It is the only ad in the batch with no `absolute-stop` or `coined-category` exposure.

**Regulated edge — all clear.** No "keeps you dry", "stay drier", "de-perspirant", "antiperspirant", "reduces sweat". The words sweat, dry and wetness do not appear in the script, the captions or any of the three ad captions. Creator Direction bans them explicitly.

**`savings-claim` — TRIGGERED. This is the one that needs sign-off.**
This ad is a cost-comparison ad, so it sits squarely in the family that already carries the account's most exposed asset (the `Aug B26` Girl Math static, with its invented £624 and £46/month). Three things keep this version defensible, and all three must hold at ship:
1. **No total saving is asserted in voice.** The spoken argument is structural ("one purchase versus a repeat purchase"), never "you save £X". Maths line 4, the only place a total could appear, is cut by default.
2. **Every variable figure is bracketed and gated.** `£[P]`, `[N]` and `[T]` cannot render without a confirmed, dated source, and alternate lines that remove them entirely are already written and are to be shot.
3. **The illustrative super is mandatory** whenever a derived figure is on screen.
→ **Needs sign-off:** a named human must confirm (a) the 22,000 source, (b) `£[P]` and `[N]` with a dated source if they are to run, and (c) the "£3" wording in caption cards 11–12 as the creator's own first-person figure rather than a competitor price claim. Until (a) resolves, the 22,000-free alternate is the shipping version.

**`quantified-claim` — handled.** "22,000" never appears bare; the card carries the unit as **times a person uses deodorant in their life**, and the full qualifying sentence is in the voice. The capacity reading — "22,000 lifetime uses", i.e. uses the bottle delivers — is a separate unsubstantiated claim and is excluded from this brief entirely. This is the specific failure already live in `HOOK 3 - MASH UP ROLLR-9X16.mp4`, and the brief is written to not repeat it.

**`competitor-brand` — clear.** The second object is an **unbranded generic** stick with no label in frame, and no competitor is named in script or caption. Note the live risk from `Aug B12`: *not naming a rival does not stop a comparison being comparative*. What keeps this one inside the line is that the compared attribute is **purchase frequency**, which is objectively verifiable and is not a denigration — unlike the "ugly / does one job badly" comparisons in the existing Mitchum and multi-pack films.

**`press-masthead` / `social-proof` — clear.** No masthead, no byline, no star rating, no review count, no award.

**No fabricated people — clear.** Every first-person line is the creator's own experience of their own purchase. No testimonial is quoted, no customer is named, no review is reproduced, no platform UI is mimicked.

**The "small British brand" line — no claim, no substantiation needed.** The adapted line is *"A small British brand decided the thing you buy forever should be bought once."* Taken from `Ad Creative/09_September/DR - Aesthetics/Design/` (Hook 1/2/3, 4:5 and 9:16), whose verbatim original is *"A small British brand decided the most used object in your bathroom should be the most beautiful."* We keep the construction — origin, deliberateness, modesty — and land the second half on this concept's argument instead of beauty, since aesthetic/design is Rollr's most over-used ground (38 of 187 hooks, eight briefs on "too beautiful to hide"). It asserts nothing measurable: no efficacy, no saving, no origin-of-manufacture claim, no superlative. **Nothing else from that film is used.** Its surrounding copy is claims-dense — one-of-a-kind gemstone, designed to keep forever, stops odour in its tracks, and a Times masthead quote — and all four are excluded. Separately: the press headline "Rollr Reimagines Deodorant As We Know It…" is third-party copy sitting in the permissions bucket with the mastheads, and is **not used anywhere in this brief**.

**Gemstone language — clear, and guarded.** No "semi-precious", "one-of-a-kind" or "carved" appears, so the £45/£35 Steel swap is safe in both directions. The shoot must still use a gemstone bottle for the £45 cut.

**`superlative` — clear.** "The last deodorant you'll ever buy" is deliberately **not** used, despite being the brand's signature and an obvious fit, for two reasons: it is a superlative needing a durability substantiation memo, and it has run across 17 briefs. C1 F7's "I just stopped buying them" is the same idea in the creator's own mouth, as a report rather than a promise.

**Verdict: clean, with one named sign-off** — the `savings-claim` substantiation above. Nothing else in this script requires a decision.
