# "Buy well, buy once" — creator script batch

Three Creator Script Ad concepts for Rollr, built from a hook structure borrowed from a
**Finisterre** Instagram ad that Milo flagged in `#rollr-wonderland` on 2026-10-24.

Four deliverables: **three creator briefs** for net-new footage, and **one finished motion ad**
cut from footage Rollr already owns (`D-motion-ad-from-library.md`, rendered to `renders/`).

All three: 9:16, single creator, one location, phone-shot, lower-third phrase-chunked captions.

## Runtime: the words are the fixed thing

These films run **31–43 seconds**, not the 17–22s an earlier draft specified. That box was
inherited from the reference ad and was never a requirement — good DR runs to 40s, and Rollr's
own films run 44–67s.

The correction came out of building an animatic rather than out of reading. A script read at a
natural pace came back at 39s against a brief claiming 20s, so every variation was measured:
the authored frame timings implied **230–342 words per minute**. The reference ad is 67 words in
17.47s — **230 wpm**, already a brisk read with no pauses — and a natural conversational delivery
measures about **160 wpm**. Several of these scripts could not have been performed at any speed.

So the runtimes were re-derived from word count, and the storyboards stretched to fit:

- **~160 wpm** is the planning rate. A word costs 0.375 seconds.
- **No beat sits on screen longer than ~3.5s.** Seven frames across 40s is a six-second average
  hold, and both flow reads of the reference put its likely drop-points at its *slowest*
  stretches — which were only 2.5s. Coverage went from 7–8 setups per variation to 17–26 beats.
- **If it needs to be longer, add shots. Do not cut lines.**

The extra room paid for itself: B2 keeps both of its proof beats, C2's written-card maths gets a
beat per number instead of being rushed, and A1 carries the origin line without anything else
coming out. The honest cost is production time — roughly 90 minutes per variation becomes two to
three hours.

## D — the one that already exists

`D-motion-ad-from-library.md` is Concept A rebuilt from library footage and **rendered**: 27s,
9:16, at `renders/Rollr_MotionAd_A_BuyWellBuyOnce_9x16.mp4`. It carries no number at all and still
closes the price frame, because the argument is one purchase versus a repeating one.

It exists because "98.1% rights-unknown" means *nobody has answered*, not that the answer is no.
Ten unrestricted clips with no burned-in text, re-captioned, re-ordered to the verified refill
mechanic. Most of it came from one previously-uncut clip, `CristinaRomano_ASMR_Twirl.mov`.

It is **ready to view and decide on, not ready to spend behind** — three creator agreements need
checking first, and it has no spoken voice, so it cannot carry the first-person testimony A, B and
C are built on.

## The reference, and what was taken from it

Finisterre, "Barents jumper" — `instagram.com/p/Dc_Gjy9McN5/`, posted 2026-09-07, flagged by
Instagram as a paid ad, 137k views, ~£120 product.

> *"They say buy well, buy once — and the Barents jumper has proved that for me."*

**Finisterre is not a competitor.** Knitwear, not deodorant: no overlap in category, purchase intent
or ad auction. That matters, because the panel's objection to borrowing is specifically about
*competitors* — based on Nick Theriot's published material the cost is delivery, since sameness
inside your own category raises CPM. Taking a **vehicle from an unrelated niche** is the sanctioned
procedure in that same corpus, not the discouraged one.

**Borrowed:** open mid-action with the product already moving on frame 1 · received wisdom stated
flat, then converted to first-person proof · a macro evidence passage in the middle third · an
objection flipped as "a true X that's not too Y, but still Z" · resolve on ritual, not a hard sell.

**Not borrowed:** Finisterre's words, their setting, their pacing — or their fault.

## The fault we are not inheriting

Two independent reads of the reference ad reached the same conclusion: **it opens a
price-justification frame and never closes it.** No price, no spec, no number on the testimony, no
reason the jumper lasts. Its comment section is a price revolt as a result, and its strongest critic
attacks the premise rather than the price:

> *"I have a jumper I bought from a fast fashion brand… still going strong 13 years later. What are
> people doing to their jumpers? 😅"*

Every concept here closes the frame it opens, and every script has to survive a viewer thinking
**"my £3 Sure works fine."** None of them dodge it; A3, B1 and C3 each take it head-on.

## Two things to settle before this shoots

**1. The 22,000 figure has no source on file.** It closes the price argument in all three concepts.
Every asset carrying it is tagged "needs a source/calculation on file"; it has run since May and now
rides in creator handwriting as well as brand voice. The derivation is probably trivial —
applications per day × years — but nobody has written it down. **If it cannot be derived, Concept A
does not ship in this form.**

Use the approved sense and wording: *"that's how many times you use deodorant in your life."*
Do **not** write "22,000 lifetime uses" — that phrasing appears once, in one creator's handwritten
list, and asserts bottle capacity, which is a separate and separately unsubstantiated claim.

**2. These are net-new shoots, not edits.** Of 1,386 tagged Rollr assets, **98.1% are
`rights: unknown`** (`brand.yml` ships `tree_rules: []`, so nothing is positively cleared for paid)
and **95.2% carry burned-in text**. The largest scene type, creator talking-head, leaves under 20
freely re-editable files, all still rights-unknown. A motion ad cut from the library is not honestly
available. Everything cited under `Reference scenes` is **look reference for the creator only**.

## Figures: locked, and withheld

**Locked:** £45 gemstone finishes (Rose Quartz, Blue Dumortierite, Green Aventurine) · £35 Steel ·
three scents. Gemstone and £45 copy must never run over Steel.

**Deliberately absent, because the account currently contradicts itself:** refill price (£6/mo,
£3.75/mo and £12 all live) · packaging reduction (90% and 95%) · Trustpilot (4.4 / 4.5 / 4.8 / 4.88 /
5.0) · design awards ("three" and "7", same month) · patents ("2 patents pending" in the ads against
the founder saying "we have a patent for" the lid lock, about a different thing) · "27 million
binned today" · sachet weight. Each appears in the briefs only as a bracketed placeholder with a
CONFIRM note. **No writer picked a number and hoped.**

## Selection note

**Do not run A3 and C3 in the same test cell.** Both answer the £3 objection in near-identical
words. The visuals are entirely different, so they are fine as separate concepts — but pick one per
cell or reword the other.

## Still open

- No human has signed any of these off. Criterion 12 is scored honestly low in all three.
- **Customer vocabulary is written, not mined.** No review or comment mine was run — the audit
  records what Rollr *said*, not what customers said back, and Rollr's real copy bank sits outside
  the walked Drive roots. Pulling the comment threads on the September price assets and swapping one
  hook for a verbatim customer line is cheap and would materially improve all three.
- **Wonderprism was not consulted.** Its deployed MCP server is failing Postgres auth after the API
  token refresh and needs its `DATABASE_URL` updated. It was the optional item; the batch does not
  depend on it, but a format survey would still be worth running afterwards.
