# What to brief next — four creator briefs, ranked

**Date:** 2026-10-24 · A recommendation for decision, not a decided plan.

Mapped against the two frameworks already in play on this account: **Meta's creative diversity
framework** (the October gap analysis) and **Dara Denney's 5 Levels of Creative Diversity**
(`aimarketingschool/experts/dara-denney/knowledge/frameworks/5-levels-of-creative-diversity.md`,
recent, 2026-08-05).

## Where October left us

From the October gap analysis (`claude.ai/artifact/HYmtSeuBnL35j65ENC7mK9`, posted to
`#rollr-wonderland` 2026-10-16):

- Spend is **concentrated in a handful of winners**.
- **Native-looking statics were almost untouched** — "that's where the best small-spend returns are".
- The **Emotional + Image + Lo-Fi quadrant was empty**; the IG-native "perfume" set was the only
  concept going into it.
- The *"enjoys their routine"* buyer was **~7% of spend**.
- **Fragrance is already the most efficient message.**

October answered those with **statics**. Every brief in that batch was a still. On Dara's ladder
that is **Level 1 — format expansion**, which she calls the lowest rung and the place most brands
stop: *"Very few unicorn ads come as a result of just changing up the format."*

**So the gap now is not format. It is Levels 2 and 4 — awareness and persona — and those need
video, which means creators.**

## Brief 1 — The wear test · "Does it actually work"

**Highest priority. This is the hole in the whole account.**

**Persona:** P2 Burned Natural Switcher (`vocabulary.json`) — *"lead with proof of efficacy, no
transition anxiety."*
**Dara level:** 2 (awareness — solution-aware comparison) + 3 (desire — security, avoiding
embarrassment).

**The case.** The copy audit found the corpus answers *"will I actually smell?"* with **assertion
only**. Duration appears three times in seven months, all seasonal, and there is **no wear test on
file anywhere**. Meanwhile duration claims are already running: Laura May's *"it lasts so well
throughout the day"* is in the ad we just cut, and `Caz_WooWoo` claims *"minerals help reduce
moisture and sweat throughout the day"* — a dryness claim that sits near antiperspirant regulation.

We are making a claim we cannot evidence. A creator-shot wear test is the cheapest way to fix both
the marketing gap and the substantiation gap at once.

**Shape:** one creator, one day, timestamped. Morning application, then real checkpoints — commute,
gym or a hot room, 6pm, end of day. No styling. The timestamps are the format.
**Why it is also defensive:** it produces the evidence file for every duration claim already live.

## Brief 2 — Men · Vetiver

**Persona:** effectively unserved. `vocabulary.json` records Vetiver & Lemon as *"most associated
with male customers"*, and the account has **two male-facing assets, both reactive**.
**Dara level:** 4 — audience and use-case expansion, which she names as the level that
*specifically beats Andromeda*, because feeding the algorithm more personas helps under
hyper-personalised delivery.

**The case.** This is a whole persona with a scent built for it and no creative. It is also the
cleanest new audience signal we can hand Meta.

**Shape:** male creator, his own bathroom, no ceremony. The object does the work — heavy glass, the
weight, the stone. Avoid the self-care register entirely; this is the one brief where the
"ritual/intentional" framing is wrong.
**Watch:** gemstone-properties language must not travel here. Steel is £35 and not a gemstone —
if the creator is given Steel, no £45 copy and no "one of a kind".

## Brief 3 — Scent as the hook

**Dara level:** 1 → 3 (format into desire). **Meta quadrant:** extends the proven Emotional + Lo-Fi
space from image into video.

**The case.** Fragrance is the account's **most efficient message** and only **3 of 187 hooks** lead
with it. October proved the angle in statics ("Your friends will ask what perfume this is"). Nobody
has made the video version.

**Shape:** open on the smell reaction, not the product. The existing footage already shows this
works — Laura May's wrist moment, Caz's *"Oh, it smells quite kind of herby"*, Chloe's *"wow, I
smell good."* Those are reactions, not descriptions, and reactions are what we are short of.
**Reuse note:** `bas transcribe --find` now makes scent reactions findable across 345 clips.

---

## Brief 4 — The refill, as the hook

**Dara level:** 1 (format) on an under-used message. **Meta:** supports the "buy once" economics the
account keeps gesturing at.

**The case.** Refill-as-hook has run **twice in seven months** despite being the entire business
model, and the index holds **13 assets with the refill in frame out of 1,386** (0.94%). The ad we
just cut leans on Caz's refill demo and it carries the film — but it is one creator's single take
from May, and it is the only good one we have.

**Shape:** the mechanic in the first two seconds, hands only, no face. Powder, water, the ball going
back on. This is also the most reusable footage we could commission: every future motion cut needs
it and currently there is one source.

## What I would not brief

- **Another aesthetic/design concept.** 38 of 187 hooks. Saturated.
- **More price defence.** October added to an already-worked angle; the wear test addresses the
  underlying objection better than another price argument.
- **Anything using the founder.** See `E-dr-the-arc.md` — it invites scrutiny a creator does not.

---

## Two things to fix alongside, neither of which is a brief

1. **Give every new creator file an `@handle` in its name.** `provenance.py` matches creators by
   `@handle` in the path. Rollr's files carry none, so **creator clearance cannot fire for any Rollr
   asset** and everything resolves to `rights: unknown` — 98.1% of the index. Every brief above
   makes that worse unless the naming changes at ingest.
2. **Write the 22,000 derivation down.** Applications per day × years. It is probably trivial, it
   has been running since May, and it is unsourced on every asset that carries it.

## Honest limits of this recommendation

- The October gap analysis is read from a Slack summary and the finals board, **not from the
  underlying Meta quadrant data**. The percentages quoted (7% of spend, "concentrated in a handful
  of winners") are Michael's, not re-derived here.
- **No performance data was consulted.** Nothing here says which existing ads worked; the angle
  counts are counts of what was *made*, from the shipped-copy audit, not of what performed.
- Dara's levels are an AI-generated advisory system built from her public material. Positions are
  phrased from that corpus and she has not reviewed any of this.
