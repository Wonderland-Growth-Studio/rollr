# Creator Script Ad — Concept A: "Buy well, buy once"

**Batch:** Buy well, buy once (3 concepts) · **This brief:** A of 3
**Format:** Creator Script Ad (net-new shoot) · 9:16 · 17–22s · burned-in lower-third captions
**Product in frame:** Rollr bottle, **gemstone finish only** (Rose Quartz / Blue Dumortierite / Green Aventurine, £45). **Not Steel.**
**Status:** ready to shoot once the two CONFIRM items in `Numbers used` are answered.

---

### Strategy

Rollr's buyer is solution-aware and the deodorant-claim market is spent, so the only rung left to win on is the mechanism — and refill-as-hook has run twice in seven months despite being the entire business model. This ad puts the refill in frame zero, hands only, no face, and converts a piece of flat British received wisdom into first-person proof in under four seconds. It then closes the price frame it opens, with the lifetime-uses figure and a visible reason the bottle survives, because the reference ad's fatal flaw was opening that frame and walking away from it.

**Belief reframe (the angle):** *You think the problem is that £45 is a lot for a deodorant. It's actually that you've been buying the same object forty times instead of once.*

**The 20% bet:** that a face-free, macro, mechanism-first open outperforms a talking head for a product nobody can picture. Everything else — the price-defence angle, the lifetime-uses argument, the creator read — is validated material already running.

**Evidentiary rationale (one sentence, Dara gate):** Rollr's own comment and copy history shows the £45 objection answered seven different ways in September alone with words and never once with the mechanism, and `shows_parts: refill` is 0.94% of 1,386 tagged assets — this is an empty persona×awareness cell, not a competitor imitation.

### Creator Direction

**Tone.** Flat, dry, slightly unbothered. The person is not selling; they are explaining something they worked out. No upward inflection at the end of lines. No "you guys". No "obsessed". If a line sounds like a brand wrote it, cut words out until it doesn't.

**Voice.** British English. Record a clean VO on the phone in the same room as the shoot so the acoustic matches — the whole film is VO over picture, there is no sync dialogue to hit.

**Setting.** A real bathroom shelf or dressing table that somebody actually uses. Daylight from one side, no lamps, no ring light. A tap in reach of the camera, and a small jug. Leave the other objects on the shelf — a toothbrush pot, a half-used face cream, a hairbrush. It should look like somewhere that owns a £3 stick as well.

**Wardrobe.** Hands and forearms only for the first two-thirds. Nothing on the wrists that rattles. Short or rolled sleeves in a plain neutral. Nails clean and unvarnished or a single plain colour — no nail art, it pulls focus off the glass. The face arrives once, late, undone, no make-up look.

**The mechanism — read this before shooting.** Rollr's refill is a **powder sachet plus water**, not a cartridge. The sequence, verified against the asset index: **snip the sachet → tip the powder in → pour water in from a jug → press the ball back in (*this is the click*) → shake → roll.** Two details that matter: the water is **poured from a jug**, not run from a tap over the bottle, and the thing that clicks is **the rollerball seating back into its housing**. Do not stage a cartridge snapping in; that product does not exist. A viewer who owns a Rollr will spot a wrong sequence instantly, and that costs more than a weak line does.

**Sound.** Mic the actions, not just the voice. The three sounds that carry this ad are the **glass set down on the shelf**, the **powder landing in the empty bottle**, and the **ball clicking back into its housing**. Record a few seconds of each in isolation so the editor can lift them under the VO. No music bed for the first 2 seconds — let the click land naked.

**Do**
- Start the camera with the action already happening. First frame is mid-motion, not a hand entering an empty frame.
- Shoot the macro passage on a surface at chest height so you can get 10–15cm from the glass.
- Let the bottle be set down heavily enough to hear it. The weight is the argument.
- Keep one continuous VO take across the whole script, then cut picture to it.
- Shoot every beat twice: once tight, once a step wider. The edit needs the options.
- Use an **unbranded generic plastic stick** if a comparison object is in frame.

**Don't**
- Don't show your face before ~13s.
- Don't open on the bottle sitting still, on a gemstone beauty shot, or on the word "beautiful" — that ground is 38 of 187 shipped hooks and it is spent.
- Don't say the price in the first six words. That is Concept C's job.
- Don't name or show Sure, Dove, Wild, Fussy, AKT, Nivea or Mitchum.
- Don't say "keeps you dry", "stay drier", "reduces sweat", "de-perspirant" or "antiperspirant" — regulated.
- Don't say "medical grade", "clinical grade", or anything about hormones or lymphatic drainage.
- Don't call Steel a gemstone and don't put £45 copy over a Steel bottle.
- Don't invent a customer, a review, a stat or an award.
- Don't use a ring light. The glass will flare and it will read as an ad.

### Claims check

Walked line by line against the banned and regulated list in `constraints.md` §4.

**Clean:**
- **No "medical grade" / "clinical grade"** — absent from all three variations and all three captions.
- **No hormone, endocrine or oestrogen claim** — absent.
- **No "lymphatic drainage"** — absent. No gua sha or massage language either.
- **No absolute "stops odour"** — the strongest efficacy phrasings used are "doesn't quit at four o'clock" (A1), "actually holds" (A2) and "just works through the day" (A3). These are durational, first-person and non-absolute.
- **No wetness / antiperspirant language** — "keeps you dry", "stay drier", "de-perspirant", "antiperspirant" and "reduces sweat" do not appear. There is no sweat imagery in the shot list either, which keeps the film clear of the `claims.health.dryness` surface entirely.
- **No efficacy superlative** — no "unmatched", no "best", no comparative performance claim against any named or unnamed product. A3 explicitly *concedes* that the cheap one works.
- **No press masthead, no journalist byline, no award** — none in script or caption.
