### Strategy

Concept A's mechanism argument, assembled entirely from existing library footage so it can go live
without a shoot. Opens on the refill in motion — powder into glass, sunlit, hands only — and closes
the price-justification frame on the cycle rather than on a number. Leads with the one thing about
Rollr nobody else can show: the bottle doesn't get used up.

### Numbers used

**None.** Every figure in the account is either contradictory or unsourced, so this cut carries no
number at all and the argument still lands — it runs on *one purchase versus a repeating one*,
which needs no arithmetic.

**The price was deliberately cut from the end card during review.** The first render ended on
"£45. Once." over the closing Chloe frame. That frame has the cap on and the stone is not
identifiable, and `brand.yml` is explicit that £45 copy must never run over **Steel**, which is
£35 and not a gemstone. Rather than guess the finish or re-cut to a Rose Quartz frame, the end card
became "One bottle. Bought once." If the price is wanted back, pair it with beat 6
(`ChloeH` 7.0–8.8), where the pale pink stone is unambiguous — then £45 is correct.

Absent by design: 22,000 · refill price · packaging % · Trustpilot · awards · patents · 27 million ·
sachet weight. See `README.md`.

### Claims check

Clean. No efficacy claim, no duration claim, no absolute "stops odour", no dryness or antiperspirant
wording, no gemstone-property language, no medical or clinical grade, no hormone or lymphatic claim,
no press masthead, no award, no fabricated person, no named competitor.

- The conventional roll-ons in beat 3 are **brand-illegible at this resolution** — Yaz's own tag
  records "brands not legible". Confirm at full resolution before shipping; if a label reads, recut
  or blur.
- "A small British brand decided…" is adapted from Rollr's own September copy and carries no claim.
- **"There's nothing in it that wears out"** is a durability statement about the object. It is the
  one line here a reviewer could ask about. Defensible on the glass-and-stone construction, but it
  should be signed off rather than assumed.

### ⚠ The rights position — read before this runs anywhere

Every clip here is **`rights: unknown`**, like 98.1% of the index, because `brand.yml` ships
`tree_rules: []` and no folder rule has been written. Unknown means **nobody has answered**, not
that the answer is no — which is why this was worth building. It is not clearance.

Before this is placed as a paid ad, three creators need checking against their agreements:
**Cristina Romano**, **Yaz Kemp**, **Chloe H**. All three sit in `ASSETS/UGC/`, none is inside a
restricted folder, and none carries an `AdUsageExpired` marker — but `core/provenance.py` matches
creators by `@handle` in the path, and Rollr's files don't carry handles, so creator clearance
**cannot currently fire for any Rollr asset**. That is a known structural gap in `brand.yml`, not a
verdict on these three.

This cut is therefore **ready to view and ready to decide on, not ready to spend behind.**

### What this proves, and what it doesn't

It proves the library can carry a complete mechanism-led ad without a shoot — the footage existed
and had never been cut this way. `CristinaRomano_ASMR_Twirl.mov` in particular is a finished
product film nobody had used.

It does not replace the creator briefs. This cut has **no spoken voice** (source audio is ASMR and
room tone, with the talking-head beats muted because their original speech says something else), so
it cannot carry the first-person testimony that A, B and C are built on — and first-person is what
makes the price argument land. **A music bed still needs laying under it.**

It is also the thinnest the library goes: of 1,386 tagged assets, **338 videos are unrestricted
with no burned-in text**, but only a handful carry clean product mechanics, and most of this film
came from one clip.

### Revision note — the first cut was incoherent, and why

The first render was rejected by the client as making no sense, and it did not. The fault was
method: the shot list was built from *which beats existed in the library*, and Concept A's caption
script was then laid over it without checking that each card matched the frame underneath. The
render was reviewed for caption legibility and for the price claim, and never for whether the words
described the picture.

Four specific breaks, all now fixed:

1. **It opened on the solution while the words described the problem.** The first six seconds were a
   hand putting powder into a jar — unreadable to anyone who did not already know Rollr — under a
   caption about subscriptions. The film now opens on the two plastic roll-ons, so the picture *is*
   the problem the line names.
2. **The refill was shown, interrupted, then narrated retroactively.** Powder and water played at
   0–6s; the edit cut away; then at 8.7s a card said "Powder in. Water on. Press the ball back in."
   over actions already five seconds gone. The mechanic now plays once, in order, as one run.
3. **Two cards were swapped.** "The bottle's glass" ran over a macro of the stone ball, and "The
   roller's stone" ran over the capped bottle. Each now sits on its own subject.
4. **One card contradicted its picture.** "There's nothing in it that wears out" played over the
   sachets — the one component that *is* consumed. That beat now reads "The only thing you replace
   is the powder", which is what the shot shows and a stronger line besides.

**The check that was missing, and is now standing procedure for any library cut:** before burning
captions, render the assembly without them, step it frame by frame, and write down what is actually
on screen in each second. Only then write the cards against that list. A caption script carried over
from a creator brief describes footage that was never shot.

### Revision note 2 — muted talking heads removed, real voice used instead

The second cut was coherent but used two talking-head beats with the sound off, which reads as
assembled rather than made. The options were to drop talking heads entirely or to use ones whose
speech actually matches the storyboard. The second turned out to be available — but only after
doing something nobody had done.

**Neither clip had a transcript record.** `YazKemp_27Million_Direct.mov` and
`ChloeH_CanYouBelieveThisIsADeodorant_Shout.mp4` were both absent from `transcripts.jsonl`, which
under `AGENTS.md` means *never listened to*, not *silent* — 147 of 416 Rollr videos are in that
state. They were being used muted because nothing in the index said they contained speech. Both
carry a full scripted voiceover. They have now been run through `bas transcribe` and the records are
committed to the index.

(`CristinaRomano_ASMR_Twirl.mov` **did** have a record, correctly logging one `(sizzling)` segment
and no speech. That is a real "no", and it is why her footage no longer appears in this cut —
beautiful, but it cannot carry a line.)

**What was deliberately left on the cutting-room floor.** Both reads contain claims this batch will
not ship:

| Cut line | Why |
|---|---|
| "27 million of these get binned every single day" | ⚠ quantified eco stat, no source on file |
| "This is the last deodorant you'll ever buy" | `watch.superlative` in `brand.yml` |
| "Four scents blended like proper perfume" | **factually wrong** — the catalogue has three; Juniper is bundles and draft listings only |
| "the gemstone roller is forever" | permanence claim |
| "it's planet friendly" | `watch.sustainability` |
| "It's heavy, made of glass and gemstone" (Yaz) | gemstone language over a ball that reads dark and may be **Steel**, which is not a gemstone |

**Claims status of what remains.** "A real gemstone rollerball / this one is the rose quartz" is
true and visibly verifiable — Chloe's ball is rose quartz and the macro shows it. Everything else is
first-person sensory or a plain description of the mechanism. Two to sign off: **"It takes 20
seconds every couple of months"** (a product-performance claim; other assets put a sachet at about
two months, so it is probably right and should still be confirmed) and **"soft and gentle on my
skin"** (brushes `health.irritation`; first-person creator experience is materially weaker exposure
than brand voice, per `health_voice_rule`).

**Note the trade-off this cut makes.** The previous version carried a written argument —
subscription, buy once, the small British brand line. This one carries the creators' argument, which
is warmer and real but less pointed, and the "small British brand" beat is gone because neither
creator says it. If the written argument matters more than the voices, the 24.8s version is in the
git history at `58120c3`.
