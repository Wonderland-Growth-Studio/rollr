### Strategy

Concept A's mechanism argument, assembled entirely from existing library footage so it can go live
without a shoot. Opens on the refill in motion — powder into glass, sunlit, hands only — and closes
the price-justification frame on the cycle rather than on a number. Leads with the one thing about
Rollr nobody else can show: the bottle doesn't get used up.

### Numbers used

**None.** Every figure in the account is either contradictory or unsourced, so this cut carries no
number at all and the argument still lands — it runs on *one purchase versus a repeating one*,
which needs no arithmetic.

**The price was deliberately cut from the end card during review.** The first render ended on
"£45. Once." over the closing Chloe frame. That frame has the cap on and the stone is not
identifiable, and `brand.yml` is explicit that £45 copy must never run over **Steel**, which is
£35 and not a gemstone. Rather than guess the finish or re-cut to a Rose Quartz frame, the end card
became "One bottle. Bought once." If the price is wanted back, pair it with beat 6
(`ChloeH` 7.0–8.8), where the pale pink stone is unambiguous — then £45 is correct.

Absent by design: 22,000 · refill price · packaging % · Trustpilot · awards · patents · 27 million ·
sachet weight. See `README.md`.

### Claims check

Clean. No efficacy claim, no duration claim, no absolute "stops odour", no dryness or antiperspirant
wording, no gemstone-property language, no medical or clinical grade, no hormone or lymphatic claim,
no press masthead, no award, no fabricated person, no named competitor.

- The conventional roll-ons in beat 3 are **brand-illegible at this resolution** — Yaz's own tag
  records "brands not legible". Confirm at full resolution before shipping; if a label reads, recut
  or blur.
- "A small British brand decided…" is adapted from Rollr's own September copy and carries no claim.
- **"There's nothing in it that wears out"** is a durability statement about the object. It is the
  one line here a reviewer could ask about. Defensible on the glass-and-stone construction, but it
  should be signed off rather than assumed.

### ⚠ The rights position — read before this runs anywhere

Every clip here is **`rights: unknown`**, like 98.1% of the index, because `brand.yml` ships
`tree_rules: []` and no folder rule has been written. Unknown means **nobody has answered**, not
that the answer is no — which is why this was worth building. It is not clearance.

Before this is placed as a paid ad, three creators need checking against their agreements:
**Cristina Romano**, **Yaz Kemp**, **Chloe H**. All three sit in `ASSETS/UGC/`, none is inside a
restricted folder, and none carries an `AdUsageExpired` marker — but `core/provenance.py` matches
creators by `@handle` in the path, and Rollr's files don't carry handles, so creator clearance
**cannot currently fire for any Rollr asset**. That is a known structural gap in `brand.yml`, not a
verdict on these three.

This cut is therefore **ready to view and ready to decide on, not ready to spend behind.**

### What this proves, and what it doesn't

It proves the library can carry a complete mechanism-led ad without a shoot — the footage existed
and had never been cut this way. `CristinaRomano_ASMR_Twirl.mov` in particular is a finished
product film nobody had used.

It does not replace the creator briefs. This cut has **no spoken voice** (source audio is ASMR and
room tone, with the talking-head beats muted because their original speech says something else), so
it cannot carry the first-person testimony that A, B and C are built on — and first-person is what
makes the price argument land. **A music bed still needs laying under it.**

It is also the thinnest the library goes: of 1,386 tagged assets, **338 videos are unrestricted
with no burned-in text**, but only a handful carry clean product mechanics, and most of this film
came from one clip.
