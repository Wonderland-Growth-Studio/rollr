## The pattern is called "the arc", and it is not mine

Named in the corpus, not invented here. Based on **Curtis Howland's** published material
(`curtis-howland`, 100-checks item 56), verbatim:

> *"Use the arc: hook, problem, solution, proof, CTA. After you name the pain, move straight to
> proof. Don't meander."*

**Stitching several ads into one is explicitly sanctioned**, under five names across the corpus —
it is a default production unit, not a fringe move. Based on **Dara Denney's** published material
(`7-lazy-formulas.md` §1), verbatim: *"(a) Stitch top-performing full video ads together.
(b) Stack 3-4 of your top-performing hooks into one video."* Alessio calls the same thing
**assembled ads** / value blocks; Nick's own neutral term is **Frankenstein ads**; Curtis has a
**Mashup/Remix** column in his diversity matrix.

Other doctrine applied:
- **Proof is two beats.** Based on Nick's material, proof is *"seasoning, not the whole bottle"*;
  based on Alessio's current material, *"medium proof slightly outperforms high proof… one
  demonstration, one trial, don't stack proof like you're defending yourself."* We run exactly two.
- **Proof sits after mechanism and immediately before the CTA** (Curtis 59).
- **Runtime 25–35s** (Alessio's 2026 five-step formula). We land at 33.4s.
- **Two creators in opposing roles, never agreeing.** Based on Lorenzo's two-actor system, the named
  failure is *"two hosts who agree with each other"* / *"you filmed a testimonial with extras."*
  Here the founder carries doubt and price; Caz carries discovery and mechanism. They are not
  saying the same thing.
- **Caption the hook unconditionally** (Alessio). Caz's hook carries her own burned caption.
- **Last frame is the offer, not the logo** (Curtis 61). The end card is a real shipped Rollr asset
  with a SHOP NOW button, not something fabricated here.

### ⚠ Be honest about V2: the corpus does not support it

Asked directly, the corpus is **silent** on withholding the opening hook, and the nearest live
doctrine runs the other way:

- Based on **Lorenzo's** current material, the named move is **"steal the hook from the middle"** —
  find the strongest later line and move it to the **front**. V2 is the exact opposite vector.
- **Curtis 51:** *"one hook, then proof… not two openings competing."*
- **Curtis 62:** design frame one as if it were the whole ad.

What *is* supported is a **re-hook mid-roll** (Alessio: *"consider a second hook at second 16"*) and
a late twist-as-second-hook (Nick). So V2 should run as a genuine experiment, not as best practice.

**A supported third arm exists if you want one — V3:** Caz's hook at frame zero *and* repeated as a
re-hook at ~0:16, which is the version the corpus actually endorses. Not built; say the word.

### Why the founder came out

V1 and V2 used the founder's price monologue as the problem beat. **That cut was brand-damaging and
is withdrawn.**

The founder says *"why would anyone spend forty-five quid on a deodorant? That's just stupid, it's
unnecessary."* His rebuttal — *"but we're not really selling deodorant; what we're selling is the
idea that you can have an elevated experience every day"* — runs from **10.3s to 21.2s** in his
source, **and the cut ended at 9.55s.** So the ad had the founder calling his own product stupid and
unnecessary, and nothing after it answered him. The creator beats that followed were about softness
and scent; they do not rebut a price objection.

Two separate faults, worth naming apart:
1. **The edit truncated an argument mid-way**, keeping the concession and dropping the answer.
2. **Using the founder at all invites scrutiny** a creator does not. A creator saying a product is
   lovely is an opinion; a founder characterising his own pricing is a statement about the business.

V3 removes him. The only thing retained from his film is the **end card**, which is a plum graphic
with the wordmark and a SHOP NOW button — no founder in frame, no founder audio. It is a shipped
brand asset being reused as a brand asset.

### Why V3 was wrong

V3 used four creators in roughly equal share: **six person-changes in 35 seconds**, four rooms, four
grades, four caption styles. It read as a compilation, or an editing error, rather than an ad.

The doctrine for this was already in `combining.md` and I did not follow it:

- Based on **Curtis Howland's** published material, his working mashup was *"**one** strong
  90-second ad stitched together with several other creator clips"* — a **spine with cutaways**, not
  a rotation.
- Based on **Lorenzo's** material, the single genuine anti-Frankenstein warning in the corpus is
  about **hook↔body continuity**.
- And *"a recombination is not diversity"* — four faces does not make a diverse ad, it makes a
  confusing one.

**The rule to carry forward: one creator owns the ad; everyone else is an insert.** Pick the spine
by who has the most usable continuous footage, not by who has the best single line. Caz wins on
that test — 69 seconds, one setup, one outfit, covering hook, product, mechanism and reaction.

Dropping to three creators also removed a caption style. Caz and Laura carry their own burned
subtitles; only Chloe needed captions adding.

### What was wrong with the joins, in numbers

The complaint was that the starts and stops felt unnatural. Measuring the seven V4 segments at
their edges said why, and it was not subjective:

| Segment | mean | first 150ms | last 150ms |
|---|---|---|---|
| s2 pour | −19.6 | −33.8 | **−71.7** |
| s6 close | −19.7 | −31.5 | −24.6 |
| s7 card | **−91.0** | −91.0 | −91.0 |

Three separate faults:

1. **A dead gap at the end of the pour** — −71.7dB is nothing at all, so the ad stopped breathing
   for a sixth of a second before the next shot started.
2. **A cliff into the end card.** s6 ended mid-level at −24.6dB and the card was *absolute digital
   silence*. That drop is the single most audible artefact in V4.
3. **Hard butt-joins throughout**, with only 0.05s fades and no crossfade, so four different rooms'
   worth of room tone switched instantly at every cut.

Three fixes, applied to both films:

- **0.25s crossfades at every join**, video and audio (`xfade` + `acrossfade`). Each segment is
  pulled with a 0.25s *handle* past its out-point so the dissolve eats handle, not speech — the
  nominal timings in the EDLs below are unchanged and no line is clipped.
- **A continuous room-tone bed** under the whole film, lifted from genuine silence in the Caz clip
  at **46.40–47.40s** (−66dB mean, real room, not generated noise), looped and raised 10dB. It
  glues four sources together and it is what removes the −91dB cliff: the card now sits at −53 to
  −64dB instead of nothing.
- **Longer fades in and a padded tail**, so the audio stream runs the full length of the video
  rather than ending 0.23s early.

Verified by re-measuring every boundary after render. No boundary now drops below the bed floor.

### V6 — why it is a different ad, not a re-edit

V5 and V6 are not two trims of one film. V5 argues **product-first**: here is a strange object,
here is what it does, here is how it feels. V6 argues **habit-first**: here is a thing you do on
autopilot, here is what it could be instead — and only once that case is made does the hook arrive
as the conclusion the viewer has already reached.

That is what moving the line does. *"Can you believe that this is a deodorant?"* at 0s is a
scroll-stopper asking a question. At 27.4s, after the mechanism, the application and two proof
beats, it is a **verdict**, and the end card lands on it.

Caz drops from 63% to 37%, which was the point, but the spine rule from V3 still holds: this is not
a rotation. **Laura May opens and proves** (two beats, 11.3s), Caz owns the mechanism block
unbroken, and Chloe and Cher are single inserts. Seven performance segments, five person-changes — but they are
grouped, not alternating, so it reads as an argument with sections rather than a compilation.

Cher Lourens comes back in V6 and only in V6. Her *"I leave it out on my shelf because it's earned
its place there"* is the one line in the library that resolves a price objection without naming a
price, and in V6 it sits exactly where that resolution is needed — immediately before the hook.
