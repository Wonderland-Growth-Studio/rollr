# The final three — C1, B3, C3

Nine storylines, two expert panels, one refinement pass. Three survive. **Every number dependency is
gone**: the 22,000 beat is lifted from C1, and B3 and C3 never carried one. Nothing in this batch
waits on a derivation — which has blocked every previous version.

Working papers: `storylines/` in the session scratchpad (nine storylines, both panel verdicts).

## Batch decisions, binding on all three

### Three lengths, not three hooks

Alessio's measured data (top 60 performers across **$20M of January 2026 Meta spend**): ads at
**25–35s averaged 55% more spend** than 41–50s; benefit delivered in **0–2s got 52% higher spend**
than 2–4s; **77% of winners used statement hooks**. All three mains run 38–44s and reach a spoken
benefit at 19–27s.

The answer is not to trim. His named anti-pattern is *"one option with four doors that all lead to
the same room"*, so each film ships as **three structurally different ads**:

| | Short (~22–25s) | Main (~40s) | Long (~60s) |
|---|---|---|---|
| For | Ready to act | Scrolling, not looking for this | Interested, needs information |
| Hook | Benefit statement, mechanism in frame 1 | Scene statement, object in frame 1 | Context statement, audit shape |
| Proof | The refill, performed | The story the object tells | Questions answered while doing it |
| Close | Price + CTA | Absence | Specifics — scents, what you keep |

The **shorts** serve Alessio's band. The **mains** stay problem-led, which is Lorenzo's shape and
Dara's growth pocket. Those two positions are directly opposed and both current — shipping both
shapes from the same concept **resolves it with data instead of averaging into a number nobody
published.**

All three mains carry a lower-third caption at **0.0–2.5s** putting a benefit on screen inside
Alessio's window without touching the opening scene.

### The £15 that saves seven films

Seven of the nine storylines contained a macro powder pour and **not one brief had a kit line for
it**. White powder into clear glass is what a phone does worst — it blows out or disappears.

> **Powder-pour kit.** Clip-on macro lens (£8–12). Matt black card behind the glass. Window **to the
> side**, never behind. **Lock exposure and focus on the glass before you pour** — do not let it
> re-expose mid-pour. Phone on a stack of books, not hand-held. One rehearsal pour, and wipe the
> glass with a microfibre first.

### Banned asks — no beat in any of the three requires these

- **No shallow depth of field a phone can't make.** Where two objects are compared it is two cuts, or
  both in focus in one frame.
- **No months of genuine handling wear.** Every scuff insert is cut — on a phone a mark on clear
  glass reads as nothing, and *a one-second insert of nothing is worse than no insert*. Age is now
  shown as **a strip of sachets with the ends torn off**, which a camera can see.
- **No camera move that should be a cut.** B3's pull-back and C3's shelf pan are now cuts.
- **No deliberately bad light.** See C3.

**Caption 0.0–2.5s:** `The one you refill instead of replacing.`

| # | dur | SEE | SAY |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 3.0 | Drawer yanked open. Seven plain unbranded white sticks, caps off or missing, **each wound up to a different height so you can see none is finished**, one furred with dust. Her hand pushes them about. No face. | *"Seven. And not one of them is empty."* |
| 2 | 6.0 | **THE CONCESSION, MOVED HERE.** She stands them on the sink edge in a row, like evidence. Two fall over. **She doesn't fix them.** | *"They all work. Three quid each, and they all work. That was never what was wrong."* |
| 3 | 5.6 | Her face first time, in the mirror, no makeup, amused at herself. **The phone is visible in her hand — deliberate.** The row still behind her. | *"I didn't choose any of these. I grabbed them, on the way past the bread."* |
| 4 | 5.3 | Wide, static. Her, the sink, the seven standing. She isn't moving. Room tone only. | *"First thing on my skin every morning. And I'd never once thought about it."* |
| 5 | 4.5 | **THE TURN.** She sweeps the row into the drawer with the side of her hand and shuts it. Clean, decisive, slightly violent. | *"I didn't need a better one. I needed to stop buying them."* |
| 6 | 6.5 | **KIT SHOT.** Bottle set down, the weight and sound of it. Sachet torn, powder into glass (**~1.6s held silent**), warm tap to the fill line, ball in, cap, two shakes. | *"This one opens. Powder, warm water, shake. The refills come through the letterbox."* |
| 7 | 3.0 | The bottle, once, plainly. **Not a hero shot** — same counter, same light as everything else. | *"Forty-five pounds. Once."* |
| 8 | 4.6 | Drawer reopened. **Empty but for a flat strip of sachets.** Same drawer, angle and light as beat 1. She closes it, lifts the bottle out of frame. Caption card. | *"The drawer's empty. First deodorant I've ever actually chosen."* |

**~38.5s.** Turn at 19.4s.

**SHORT (~22s)** — benefit-first, names the stone (the main never does), CTA close instead of an
absence close. **LONG (~60s)** — the drawer bookends an *objection-answering* shape: one unhurried
near-real-time refill while she answers the three things she actually wondered (*"I thought it'd be a
faff" · "I thought I'd forget to order" · "I thought I'd break the glass"*), then the three scents
named plainly, then the price once.

### Share / Prioritize / Release

> **SHARE.** Almost everyone has a drawer with three-to-eight half-used deodorants in it. Nobody
> decided to have that. You never chose any of them — you grabbed one on the way past something else,
> four seconds, no thought. The thing that goes on your skin before anything else every morning is
> the one object in your house you've never made a decision about.
>
> **PRIORITIZE.** (1) Open on **your own real drawer with the real number in it** — count them out
> loud in the first three seconds. If you have four, say four. (2) **Concede before you sell** — say
> early that the cheap ones work. (3) **Diagnose yourself, not the product.** (4) Show the refill end
> to end in one go. (5) **End on the empty drawer, not the bottle.**
>
> **RELEASE.** Your drawer, your room, your number, your words. Swap the drawer for a washbag, a
> shelf, a box under the sink. **Do not read anything from this page aloud.** Ten creators, ten
> drawers, ten films.

### Production

Seven sticks, labels off, **each wound to a different height** — that is what makes "not one of them
is empty" a fact rather than a line. **Dust:** a pinch of talc on a dry make-up brush, blown off, shot
in **raking side light** — dust doesn't read in flat light. Not flour; it clumps.

**The phone in the mirror — DECIDED: visible.** No hiding it behind a towel, no cropping, **no ring
light** — a ring light in the mirror is the thing that would look wrong, not the phone.

**Fixed shoot order:** tape the phone position → shoot beat 1 (full drawer) → **immediately** empty
it, lay the sachet strip in, shoot beat 8 from the identical mark → then beats 2–7. If the reprise
can't be back-to-back it must be within 30 minutes with nothing moved. **Matching it later by eye
will not match — panel 2 killed C2 for exactly that failure.**

**~43.5s, held deliberately.** Beat 3 is the best moment in the nine by both panels and the only beat
an amateur can genuinely perform. **Trimming it to hit 35s would be buying a runtime by spending the
thing that makes the film work** — the short serves that band instead.

### Share / Prioritize / Release

> **SHARE.** Hardly anything you own can be opened any more. Sealed, glued, one-piece, use-it-up-and-
> buy-another. You're not allowed inside the things you paid for. Ours comes apart in your hands.
> The feeling isn't thrift and isn't convenience — **it's being trusted with something you own.**
>
> **PRIORITIZE.** (1) **Say "deodorant" in the first five words** and have the thing coming apart in
> frame 1 — nobody should have to guess what they're looking at. (2) **Fail to open the cheap one on
> camera, for real, and don't rush it** — the failure is funnier and more convincing the longer it's
> awkward. (3) Concede out loud that the cheap one works. (4) Say the ownership line in your own
> words — the point isn't that it's cheaper, it's that you get to keep it.
>
> **RELEASE.** Which objects you try to open, how many, what room, what order, your words throughout.
> **Do not read anything from this page aloud.**

### Production

The plain stick must be one that **genuinely does not open** — check before the day; an obvious
twist-base defeats the beat. The sachet strip must have **real torn ends**, not a neat perforation.
Shelf dressing is **her things, not styled props**.

**Beat 2 is a CUT.** Shoot tight, stop, reframe wide, shoot again. **Beat 3: shoot 30 seconds of it.**
Don't act frustration — genuinely try twice and give up. Silence does the work. **No scuff insert —
do not shoot one.**

**Age claim is casting-conditional.** If she genuinely bought hers in April and has genuinely
refilled it, *"Same glass I bought in April"* may run. **If not, the sachet-strip line only. No
invented ownership history.**

### Honest caveat on releasing this one

Panel 1 named C1 and B3 as the two that survive the script being taken away. **C3 does not survive as
cleanly** — the matched first/last frame and the match-cut bin loop are *directing decisions, not
instructions*. **So C3 is the one to run scripted**, with 3–4 creators rather than ten.

### Resolving the Steel / £35 confound

C3 was the only script on a different product at a different price, so a win could not be attributed.

1. **The £45 gemstone build enters the comparison cell.** Product and price then hold constant across
   C1, B3 and C3, so the only variable is the angle — the question the cell was built to answer.
2. **Steel is tested as its own variable, in its own cell, script held constant.** Shoot a matched
   Steel version in the same session, same creator, same room, same setups — only the bottle, the
   price line and the stone language differ. That isolates product-and-price cleanly, which the
   original construction never could.

**Call-sheet rules:** shoot the £45 version first and wrap it completely, then **new folder
`C3_STEEL_35`**, change the bottle, re-shoot only the product beats. **Separate card folders are
mandatory** — if both bottles are shot in one session an editor will mix them. No gemstone, "stone",
"semi-precious" or "carved" anywhere in the Steel cut, and **no gemstone footage cut into it**. The
two versions must never be delivered in the same file bundle.

### Production

**No low light anywhere.** Expose for her face, never for mood — *deliberate bad light is the most
expensive look there is, because cheap cameras in low light look broken rather than moody.*

**Beat 1/8:** tape the position, shoot beat 1, then **immediately shoot beat 8 without moving
anything.** Cheapest fix available and the thing that makes the film work.

**Beat 5, read twice:** one locked overhead frame. Do not move it. Do not change the light. Do not
open the curtains between drops. Four drops, same stick, hand entering from the same corner at the
same height. **If the frame or light changes between drops the gag stops working and reads as a
continuity error.** Four minutes to shoot.

Buy one object **with a genuine supermarket price sticker and leave it on** — the single best prop in
the film. Reline the bin before the loop so each drop sounds the same. **No competitor branding
visible on any shelf object — a visible logo is a legal surface, not an aesthetic one.**

## What still holds, and what this pass did not fix

**Holds.** Zero number dependencies. The diversity gate holds on the **mains** — muted, C1 is a static
drawer and a sink, B3 is two objects in two hands in daylight, C3 is a morning sequence with a locked
overhead bin loop. Three different films.

**Weaker on the shorts**, which all converge on "mechanism performed, price stated". An accepted cost
of serving the 25–35s band. If the three shorts read as one ad in a squint test on real thumbnails,
**rebuild C3's short first** — the bin loop is the most distinctive device.

**Not fixed, and nobody should pretend otherwise:**

- **This is still one persona.** A woman, 25–40, British, alone, in a domestic interior. Zero men,
  zero households, zero over-50s, zero gifting, zero second use case, **zero statics**. Dara's Level 3
  and Level 4 failures are untouched. **If a fourth slot exists it should not be a fourth film of this
  kind** — her vote was a static, a partnership ad, or a second persona.
- **Curtis's point stands.** Nine storylines existed to answer one objection, and the writers conceded
  Rollr is not cheaper on arithmetic. *"Most paid media problems are creative, pricing, margin or
  organizational problems wearing a paid media mask."* These three are the best films available from
  the brief that was set; they are not an answer to whether the offer is the binding constraint.

## Open CONFIRMs — the same four on all three

- **`LANDING PAGE`** — Barry's link 3, and the highest-value unblocked job before spend. All three end
  on an absence or an ordinary shelf. **If the click lands on a PDP whose hero is a lit gemstone
  bottle at £45, the frame reopens on the page and the price revolt happens there instead.** Nobody
  has specified where these land.
- **`CAPTION CARD`** — `£45 once · vs. a stick every [X] weeks = £[CONFIRM] over [CONFIRM] years`
  needs a calculation on file. **Do not pick a number.** Unresolved, the card ships as `£45 · once`.
- **`CASTING`** (B3 only) — is there a genuine owner-since-April creator? Determines whether the dated
  line can run at all.
- **`OPS`** — comment-moderation keyword blocklist live before spend, built from the reference ad's
  actual price-revolt language. That comment section is unmined objection research and none of the
  nine storylines showed evidence of having read it.
