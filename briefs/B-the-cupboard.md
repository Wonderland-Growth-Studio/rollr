# Creator Script Ad — Concept B: "The cupboard"

**Brand:** Rollr · **Batch:** "buy well, buy once" (3 of 3 concepts) · **Date:** 2026-10-24
**Spec:** 9:16, **32–42s depending on variation**, single creator, single location, natural light, phone-shot
**Borrowed vehicle:** Finisterre "Barents jumper" (IG `Dc_Gjy9McN5`, 2026-09-07) — **structure only**

---

### Strategy

Rollr's buyer is solution-aware, so this ad opens on the failed alternative rather than on odour: a
cupboard opened into the lens and a real, counted pile of half-used plastic sticks. The viewer is
asked to picture their own cupboard first, then shown one object that ends the cycle — and the price
frame the ad opens is closed on screen, not by totting up what the pile cost, but by pricing the
habit the pile proves: £45 once against buying the next one, and the one after that, for the rest of
your life. The one bet is that a personal, spoken *count* ("eleven") out-performs the abstract
category statistics Rollr has already run five times.

---

### Visual Reference

**The borrow, named.** Bucket: **vehicle / format**, plus **hook structure**. Source: Finisterre,
knitwear — an **indirect, non-competing** advertiser (different category, different auction, no
purchase-intent overlap), which is the permitted side of the panel's rule. What we take:

- Open mid-action with the subject already moving on frame 1.
- A received idea stated flat, then converted immediately into first-person proof.
- A macro "evidence" passage in the middle third.
- An objection flipped in the form *"a true X that's not too Y, but still Z"*.
- Resolve on habit, not on a hard sell. Karaoke captions, phrase-chunked, hard in/out.

What we **do not** take: Finisterre's words, their "buy well, buy once" line, their setting, their
pacing, or their fault. The reference ad opens a price-justification frame and never closes it, and
its comment section is a price revolt as a result. **This ad closes it** — on screen, with a unit,
inside the last six seconds.

**The look.** Domestic interior, lived-in, slightly untidy. Mixed light: a bathroom window plus the
bulb that's actually in the room. Handheld, breathing, not stabilised. Real mess, not styled mess —
a toothpaste tube on its side is correct; a curated vignette of three amber bottles is wrong. The
cupboard door is the proscenium: it opens into the lens and the pile is behind it.

### Creator Direction

**Tone.** Flat, faintly embarrassed, amused at yourself. This is a confession, not an announcement.
Think "look what I found behind the sink", not "let me tell you about my new deodorant". No rising
inflection at the end of sentences, no "you guys", no hand-on-heart sincerity. The funniest reading
is the correct one.

**Setting.** Your own bathroom or bedroom — the real one. A cupboard under the sink, a mirrored
cabinet, a bedside drawer or a washbag. One room, one session, daylight if you can get it.

**Wardrobe.** What you'd actually be wearing at 7am. Plain top, no logos, no jewellery that clatters
on the glass. Hair as it is. No makeup look, no "getting ready" sequence — that is Concept A's
territory and it is not this.

**Do:**
- **Count your real sticks before you shoot and say the real number.** If it's six, say six. If it's
  fourteen, say fourteen. The number in the script is a placeholder for yours.
- Open the cupboard/drawer **towards the lens**, so the door passes across frame and reveals.
- Let the sticks be heard — plastic knocking on plastic is the whole soundtrack of the first second.
- Pick them up. Rattle one. Read nothing off them.
- Hold the Rollr bottle like it's heavy, because it is. Let the glass land on the shelf with a sound.
- Say the price out loud without apologising for it.

**Don't:**
- **Do not show a named brand.** Labels turned to the wall, or use the unbranded generic sticks
  supplied in the kit. A competitor name legible in frame is a legal problem, not a styling choice.
- Don't tidy the cupboard first.
- Don't say the sticks "didn't work". They worked. That is the point and the script says so.
- Don't claim Rollr keeps you dry, stops sweat, or is medical/clinical anything.
- Don't mention gemstone healing properties, chakras, hormones or lymph.
- Don't name a scent count other than three.
- Don't open on the bottle. The bottle arrives around a third of the way in (B1 ~12s) and not before.

### UGC Script

**Runtime and rate — read this before changing a line.**

- **Target runtime: 32–42s**, by variation. The 17–22s box came from the reference ad and is not a
  requirement: good DR runs to 40s and Rollr's own films run 44–67s.
- **Delivery rate: ~160 wpm measured** — a relaxed, conversational read with air around the lines.
  That is the register this concept is written in. The reference's brisk 230 wpm is not.
- **Current counts:** B1 **86 words → ~32.5s** · B2 **113 words → ~42.5s** · B3 **101 words → ~39s**.
- **No beat on screen longer than ~3.5s.** Every Frame below breaks into several shots in the
  *Shot list*. **If you need more time, add shots — do not cut lines.** A six-second hold is where
  attention dies; both flow reads of the reference put its likely drop points on its slowest
  stretches, and those were only 2.5s.

Three variations. They differ in the **hook and its visual** — a cupboard, a washbag and a drawer —
not in synonyms. Pick one per creator; do not shoot all three with the same person in the same room.

**The "small British brand" beat runs in B1 only.** Rollr's own September line — *"A small British
brand decided the most used object in your bathroom should be the most beautiful"* (`Ad Creative/09_September/DR - Aesthetics/Design/`,
Hook 1/2/3) — is adapted here, not pasted. The construction that does the work is kept (origin,
deliberateness, modesty); the second half is replaced, because "should be the most beautiful" re-runs
the aesthetic/design angle, which is Rollr's most over-used ground at 38 of 187 hooks across eight
briefs. It lands on this concept's argument instead: *the thing you use every day shouldn't be the one
you keep replacing.* It is also **reported, not declaimed** — "apparently some small British brand
decided…" — because a clean brand statement would break the flat, faintly-embarrassed register the
rest of B1 is written in.

**Why B1 and not the others.** B1 is the variation that most needs it: it resolves onto a bottle on a
shelf with nothing to say who made it, and its frame 5 macro is already a pause in the action, so a
credibility beat costs it nothing. **B2 does not carry it** because its back half is already doing a
different job — the refill sachet *and* the sealed-lid travel argument, both of which it now has room
for at 42s — and a third idea in that stretch would blunt all three. **B3 does not carry it** because its whole close is the drawer
shutting, a physical punchline that only works if nothing is said over it, and because B3 is the one
variation that names both price tiers and so has the most words to carry already. Adding the beat to
all three would also make them more alike, and the diversity gate outranks the extra credibility.

### Ad caption

**Primary**

> Eleven. That's how many half-used deodorants were under my sink, and the maddening part is that
> they all worked. That's the whole business model — you finish it, you bin it, you buy it again.
> Rollr is a faceted glass bottle you keep and top up with powder refills. £45 for the gemstone
> rollers, £35 for steel. Three scents, blended by perfumers. More than one stick, obviously. But
> only once, and eleven is just the ones I've got through so far. 🔗 Shop Rollr

**Alt A — shorter, flatter**

> Go and count the deodorants in your bathroom cupboard. I'll wait. Mine was eleven, all half used,
> all of them working fine, all of them destined for the bin. Rollr is glass, you refill it with
> powder, and it's the one that stops the cycle. £45. One time.

**Alt B — leads on the arithmetic**

> I'd never counted before. Eleven sticks under the sink, not one of them finished, and that's only
> the ones I haven't thrown out yet. That's the bit that got me — not what they cost, but that there
> is no end to them. Rollr is faceted glass with a gemstone roller: you keep the bottle and refill it
> with powder in a paper sachet. £45, once. Steel is £35.

### Claims check

Walked line by line against §4 of the constraints pack.

**Clean.**

- **No banned language.** Nothing in any variation says "medical grade" or "clinical grade". No
  hormone, endocrine or oestrogen claim. No "lymphatic drainage". No absolute "stops odour" — in
  fact the script never makes an efficacy claim for Rollr at all.
- **No regulated wetness claim.** Nothing says "keeps you dry", "stay drier", "de-perspirant",
  "antiperspirant" or "reduces sweat". This was a live risk in B2 ("travel deodorant that isn't a
  compromise") and the line was written to be about **leaking in a bag**, not about perspiration.
  If a creator improvises "keeps me dry", the take is unusable.
- **No efficacy superlative.** No "unmatched", no "actually works", no comparison of performance.
- **No gemstone property language.** No healing, calming, balancing, clarity or focus claims. The
  stone appears as a physical object and a price tier only. B3 names both £35 and £45 so it is safe
  over Steel; B1 and B2 name only £45 and are therefore **gemstone-finish only** — flagged in the
  Numbers table and again here.
- **No press mastheads, no journalist bylines, no awards, no star ratings.**
- **No fabricated people.** Every testimonial beat is the creator's own first-person experience of
  their own bathroom. No named customer, no invented review, no Reddit reply, no screenshot.
- **No competitor named or shown.** This is the sharpest surface in the concept and it is handled
  three ways: unbranded generic sticks supplied in the kit, labels turned away as a fallback, and a
  "don't" in Creator Direction. The existing S02 comparison assets already use an unbranded generic,
  which is the defensible construction we are matching.

- **The "small British brand" beat (B1 only) carries no claim.** Origin, intent and modesty, and
  nothing else: no efficacy, no materials, no longevity, no superlative, no award, no masthead. It
  needs no substantiation beyond Rollr being a small British brand. **Nothing else from that
  September film travels with it** — the `DR - Aesthetics/Design` copy is claims-dense (one-of-a-kind
  gemstone, designed to keep forever, stops odour in its tracks, a Times masthead) and all of it is
  out of scope here. One line, nothing else. The third-party press headline "Rollr Reimagines
  Deodorant As We Know It…" is **not** Rollr's copy, sits in the permissions bucket, and is used
  nowhere in this brief.

**Needs sign-off before this ships.**

1. **The waste and eco framing.** The script deliberately carries **no eco figure** — no packaging
   percentage, no bin count, no "zero waste", no recyclability claim. The waste idea is carried
   entirely by a visible pile of the creator's own plastic and the words "you bin it". That is an
   observation about the creator's own behaviour, not an environmental claim about Rollr, and it is
   the reason this brief needs no eco substantiation. **Confirm legal agrees with that reading**
   before shoot, because the imagery is unmistakably an environmental argument even though the words
   are not. If legal wants it tightened, the fix is to cut "you bin it, you buy it again" to "you buy
   it again" — do not replace it with a figure.
2. **"It doesn't get used up" / "the one that stops the cycle"** (B1 frame 5, caption Alt A). This is
   a durability claim in the same family as the reference ad's unsupported "built to last for years",
   and it is the claim a sceptic will attack. It is supported in frame by the glass bottle and the
   refill mechanic, which is better than the reference managed, but **someone must sign off that
   "you keep the bottle indefinitely" is a claim Rollr stands behind** — i.e. that there is a
   warranty, a replacement policy or at minimum no known wear-out. If not, soften to "it doesn't get
   thrown away".
3. **The price argument — revised, and now cleaner than it was.** The earlier draft argued that the
   pile had already cost more than the bottle. That was arithmetically backwards (eleven supermarket
   sticks at ~£3 is £33, against a £45 bottle) and it only held if the pile were premium naturals,
   which contradicts the unbranded-cheap-stock styling note. It is removed. The script now states one
   true price, concedes plainly that it is more than one stick, and makes the comparison **forward**:
   one bottle once, against a habit with no end. **No £-per-month figure, no saving figure, no totted
   total**, which keeps it clear of `savings-claim` in `brand.yml` and means nothing here needs
   substantiating beyond the price itself. Creator still confirms their own count on camera-day.
4. **Finish/price pairing at edit.** A compliance check at the edit stage: does the bottle visible
   on screen match the price spoken? £45 over a Steel bottle is wrong twice over.
